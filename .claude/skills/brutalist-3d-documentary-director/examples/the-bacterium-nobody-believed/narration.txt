For most of the twentieth century, doctors were certain. Stomach ulcers came from stress, spicy food, and too much acid. Nothing, they believed, could live inside the stomach.

In Perth, Australia, pathologist Robin Warren kept seeing something under the microscope. Spiral bacteria, living in the stomach lining. With Barry Marshall, he argued these bacteria caused the disease.

The medical world rejected them.

So in 1984, Marshall drank a culture of the bacteria himself. Within days, he fell ill.

In 2005, Warren and Marshall received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Sometimes, the truth hides in the one place everyone agrees nothing can live.
