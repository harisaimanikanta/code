# Research: The Killer Found Through a Cousin's DNA (Golden State Killer)

- **Slug:** golden-state-killer-genealogy · **Researched:** 2026-11-21 · **Verdict:** GO (sensitive: real victims; no crime detail)
- **Period:** 1974-1986 crimes; DNA sample from 1980; arrest April 2018; guilty plea June 2020 · **Place:** California
- **Main people:** Joseph James DeAngelo (the killer); investigators incl. Paul Holes; genetic genealogist Barbara Rae-Venter (not named in script)

## Short factual summary
A serial offender known as the Golden State Killer committed at least 13 murders and dozens of rapes and burglaries across California between the 1970s and 1986, then stopped. Crime-scene DNA (including a sample from the 1980 Ventura County murders of Lyman and Charlene Smith) matched no police database. In 2018 investigators uploaded the profile to the free genealogy site GEDmatch (and used other databases), found distant relatives (initially third cousins; a second cousin via MyHeritage), built family trees back to the relatives' great-great-great-grandparents and worked forward, narrowing by age, sex and location to Joseph DeAngelo, 72, of Citrus Heights near Sacramento. On 18 April 2018 officers swabbed his car door handle while he shopped at a Hobby Lobby in Roseville; a tissue from his trash gave a second sample. Both matched. He was arrested on 24 April 2018. He had been a police officer (Exeter, then Auburn) until he was fired in 1979 after being caught shoplifting a hammer and dog repellent. He pleaded guilty to 13 murders in June 2020 and got life without parole.
