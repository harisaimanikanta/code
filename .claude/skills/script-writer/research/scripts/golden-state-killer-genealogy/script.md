## A. Story research
- **Period:** 1970s-1986 crimes; arrest 2018 · **Place:** California · **Named people:** Joseph DeAngelo only
- **Summary:** DNA from the crime scenes matched no police list. In 2018 detectives put it on a free family-tree website, found distant cousins, built family trees forward from great-great-grandparents, narrowed to one man, swabbed his car door handle at a craft store and got a match. He was a former police officer.
- **Avoided:** crime details, victims' names, rape counts, the claim that he "never took a DNA test" (unknown).

### Draft 1
Police had a killer's DNA for almost forty years. They just couldn't put a name on it.
So how did they finally find him? His cousins told them. Without even knowing it.
In the nineteen seventies and eighties, a man broke into homes all over California. He killed at least thirteen people and attacked dozens more. Then he just stopped.
He left his DNA behind. But police had a problem. DNA only gives you a name if that person is already in a police database. He wasn't.
So in two thousand eighteen, investigators tried something new. They uploaded his DNA to a free family-tree website. The kind people use to find long-lost relatives.
Nobody there was him. But some people shared a little of his DNA. Distant cousins.
Now here's the trick. Cousins share the same great-great-grandparents. So the team built huge family trees, going back more than a hundred years, and then followed every branch forward.
That gave them a long list of names. Then they crossed off everyone who was the wrong age, lived in the wrong place, or wasn't a man.
One name was left. Joseph DeAngelo. Seventy-two years old. Living quietly near Sacramento.
But they still needed proof. So while he shopped at a craft store, officers swabbed the handle of his car door.
It matched.
And the man they arrested? He used to be a police officer.
He never took a DNA test in his life. He didn't have to. His cousins took it for him.
