## A. Story research
- **Title:** Why People Used to Eat Mummies 🤮 · **Period:** 12th c. to 1924 · **Main characters:** none named
- **Summary:** "Mumia" was a black bitumen used in Persian medicine. Medieval translators wrote that it came from embalmed bodies,
  so Europe spent centuries eating ground-up Egyptian mummies. Fakes were made from fresh corpses; Merck still sold mummy powder in 1924.
  The English word "mummy" first meant the medicine.
- **Verified / disputed / sources:** see `research.md` (verdict: GO WITH QUALIFIERS).

## B. Hook options
**The question this video answers:** Why would doctors feed people dead Egyptians?
**Answer:** a translation mistake - the medicine was rock tar, and translators said it came from bodies.
**Surprise:** nobody wanted the bodies at all. **Planted detail:** "mumia, a black, sticky tar" + "bodies coated in black, sticky resin".
**Second reveal:** the word "mummy" first meant the medicine, not the body (+ Merck sold it until 1924).

### Critic pass on Draft 3 (§7b)
1. **Best unused true detail:** Francis I of France reportedly used mummy (Sugg) - single-scholar claim; doesn't beat any line. Merck's 1924 price (12 gold marks a kilo) - the year alone is stronger by ear.
2. **Weakest 5 s:** 11-16 s (what mumia is, Persian doctors). Deliberate trade-off: it plants the "black, sticky" detail the payoff needs. Kept to two short lines.
3. **Famous name:** none needed; "Egyptian mummies" is the search term and is in the first line.
4. **Feeling:** disgust (grind, feed, fresh corpses) + irony (it was tar all along).
5. **Viewer connection:** every viewer uses the word "mummy" - the final line changes it for them.
6. **Stranger test:** "The word mummy first meant medicine" is a repeatable fact.
Honest critic rating: Hook 9 (stakes 1/2 - no fix without a weakly sourced claim), Explanation 9 (11-16 s is a slight sag), Payoff 10.
