## Short factual summary
"Mumia" (Arabic mūmiyā, from Persian mūm, "wax") was a natural black bitumen that Persian and Arab physicians used as medicine.
In the 11th-12th centuries, translators into Latin (Gerard of Cremona among them) described mumia as a substance found in or exuded
from embalmed bodies in Egyptian tombs. Europeans then ground real Egyptian mummies into medicine; demand led to forgeries made from
recent corpses. Merck in Darmstadt still sold "Mumia vera Aegyptiaca" into the 20th century (price list of 1924). In English, the
word "mummy" first meant the medicinal preparation (c.1400); the "preserved body" sense came later (17th c.).

## Evidence buckets
### VERIFIED FACTS
- Mumia originally meant a bitumen; word from Persian mūm "wax" - 4, 8
- 11th-12th c. translators misidentified mumia as a substance from embalmed bodies - 1 (citing Dannenfeldt), 4
- Ground mummy sold as medicine for centuries in Europe, for bruises, epilepsy/seizures, coughs etc. - 2, 3, 4, 7
- Some Egyptian mummies are coated in black resins/bitumen, which resembled mumia - 4
- Merck sold "Mumia vera Aegyptiaca" into the 20th century; 1924 price list: 12 gold marks/kg - 5
- English "mummy" earliest sense = medicinal preparation (c.1400); body sense later - 8 (M-W, Dictionary.com/OED)
### REPORTED CLAIMS
- Guy de la Fontaine (1564) found Alexandrian sellers making "mummies" from recent corpses - via Ambroise Paré 1582 (9, 10). Script: "some sellers faked mummies from fresh corpses" (general; widely corroborated that fakes existed - 2).
- Francis I of France used mummy - Sugg (6). Not in script.
### HISTORICAL DISPUTES
- End date: "18th century" vs "late 19th" vs Merck 1924. Script uses only the Merck 1924 fact.
- Headache use: listed in some pharmacopeias, but headache cures often used powdered skull instead - headache claim removed from hook.
### POPULAR LEGENDS
- Napoleonic-era mummy paint/fuel stories - not used.
### UNKNOWN
- How much "mummy" sold was genuinely Egyptian.

## Script-safety notes
- Plain fact: mumia = tar from rocks; translation mistake; bodies ground into medicine; Merck 1924; word origin.
- Attribute/soften: forgeries ("some sellers"); uses ("bruises, coughs and even seizures").
- Avoid: headaches as the main use; "Francis I carried it in a pouch"; exact end date of the practice.
