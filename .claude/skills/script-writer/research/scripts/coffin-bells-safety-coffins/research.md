# Research: Did Coffin Bells Ever Save Anyone?
- **Slug:** coffin-bells-safety-coffins · **Researched:** 2026-11-24 · **Verdict:** GO WITH QUALIFIERS
- **Period:** 1790s-1890s · **Place:** Germany, Britain, USA · **Main people:** Duke Ferdinand of Brunswick (d. 1792), Dr Adolf Gutsmuth (1822) - not named in script

## Summary
Fear of premature burial produced "safety coffins". Duke Ferdinand of Brunswick's coffin had a window, an air tube and a lock, and he was buried with keys to the lid and the vault. In 1822 Dr Adolf Gutsmuth had himself buried alive to demonstrate his design and was fed soup, beer and sausages through a tube. Many later designs ran a cord from the body to a bell above ground; 30+ designs were patented in Germany alone in the second half of the 19th century. There is no documented case of anyone saved, and bloating/shifting during decomposition could pull the cords (false alarms). "Saved by the bell" is boxing slang (in print by c.1891), not from coffins.
