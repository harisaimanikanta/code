Did a bell on a grave ever save someone who was buried alive?
Two hundred years ago, people were terrified of waking up inside a coffin.

But there was a creepy problem.
When a body rots, it swells and moves.
That can pull the string too.
So the bell could ring for someone who was already dead.
So how many people did the bells really save?
Not one that anyone can prove.
And the saying saved by the bell? It comes from boxing, not coffins.

### Draft 1
Did a bell on a grave ever save someone who was buried alive?
Two hundred years ago, people were terrified of waking up inside a coffin.
So they got creative.
One duke built his coffin with a window, an air tube, and a lock. He was buried with the keys in his pocket.
A German doctor went even further. He buried himself alive to test his coffin, and ate soup and sausages through a feeding tube.
Then came the bells.
A string ran from the dead person's hand, up through a pipe, to a bell above the grave. One pull, and a guard would hear it.
Inventors patented dozens of coffins like these.
So how many people did the bells save?
Not one, as far as anyone can prove.
And here's the creepy part. As a body rots, it swells and shifts. That could pull the string.
So the bell could ring for someone who was already dead.
And the saying saved by the bell? It comes from boxing.
