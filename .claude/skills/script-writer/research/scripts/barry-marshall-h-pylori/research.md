## Short factual summary
In 1979 pathologist Robin Warren saw spiral bacteria in stomach biopsies wherever there was inflammation. With Barry Marshall he linked
the bacterium (later *Helicobacter pylori*) to gastritis and ulcers, against the prevailing view that ulcers came from stress and acid and
that the stomach was effectively sterile. Their abstract for the Gastroenterological Society of Australia's 1983 meeting was rejected.
After failing to infect piglets, Marshall had a baseline endoscopy and in 1984 drank a culture of the bacterium. He developed acute
gastritis with vomiting and halitosis, which was confirmed by endoscopy and biopsy. He published it in the *Medical Journal of Australia* (1985),
writing in the third person out of embarrassment. Warren and Marshall shared the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

## Evidence buckets

- Warren observed spiral bacteria in gastric biopsies in 1979 (Nobel lecture: work began June 1979). - S6, S8
- Prevailing belief: ulcers from stress/excess acid; stomach considered sterile because of acid. - S6
- Abstract submitted to the Gastroenterological Society of Australia (Feb 1983 meeting) was rejected; the rejection letter is quoted by Adrian Lee. - S7, S1
- Failed attempts to infect piglets (1984). - S1
- Baseline endoscopy, then drank cultured H. pylori; acute illness with vomiting and halitosis. - S1, S2, S5
- Published in MJA 1985; one of MJA's most cited papers. - S5
- Wrote it in the third person because he was embarrassed it was a self-experiment. - S5 (Marshall's own words)
- Nobel Prize 2005 shared with Warren ("with tenacity and a prepared mind, challenged prevailing dogmas"). - S4, S6
- Mechanism: H. pylori produces urease → ammonia that neutralises acid locally; colonises the gastric mucus layer. - standard microbiology (S6 context; textbook)
