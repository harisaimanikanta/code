# The Doctor Who Drank the Cure - Shorts Package

Format: **True Mechanism Parable** · 2026-11-19 · From IDEA CARD (idea-creator batch 2026-11-19-mixed #1) · Research: `research.md`

## A. Story research
- **Period:** 1979-2005 · **Place:** Perth, Western Australia · **Characters:** Barry Marshall (physician), Robin Warren (pathologist)
- **Summary:** Warren found spiral bacteria in inflamed stomachs (1979). The pair's conference abstract was rejected (1983); piglet infections failed; Marshall drank a culture of the bacterium (1984), got acute gastritis with the same bacteria, and published it in the third person (MJA 1985). Nobel Prize 2005.
- **Verified:** all beats used in the script. **Reported:** "bottom 10%" ranking. **Avoided:** the wife/antibiotics story, "gave himself an ulcer", "laughed at".
- **Sources:** Wikipedia (leads), Lindau Nobel, Lasker Foundation, MJA 2005 and 2014, Australian Academy of Science, Adrian Lee (RSNSW), Warren's Nobel lecture.

## C. Final voiceover script (218 words · ~62 s at 3.5 words/s)
<!-- VOICEOVER START -->
He drank a broth full of live stomach bacteria.
On purpose. To win an argument.
You see, it's the early 1980s in Perth, Australia. Barry Marshall is a young doctor with a theory almost nobody takes seriously.
Doctors are taught that ulcers come from stress and acid, and that nothing can live inside a stomach anyway.
But his colleague, a pathologist named Robin Warren, keeps finding tiny spiral bacteria in sick patients' stomachs.
So they send their findings to Australia's big gut-doctor conference.
Rejected. Ranked in the bottom ten percent.
Marshall tries to infect piglets to prove it. The piglets won't get sick.
So he becomes the piglet.
A camera down his throat shows a healthy stomach. Then he drinks the bacteria.
Days later, he's vomiting. His breath is foul.
Another camera. His stomach lining is inflamed and crawling with the same spiral bacteria.
He'd caught the infection the experts said couldn't exist.
Twenty-one years later, he and Warren win the Nobel Prize.
The bug's trick? It makes ammonia, a tiny cloud of antacid around itself, and hides in the mucus where acid can't reach.
Antacids only hid the pain. Antibiotics clear the infection.
And the paper where he proved it? He wrote it in the third person.
He was too embarrassed to admit the patient was him.
<!-- VOICEOVER END -->

## E. Editing direction
- **Pace:** a cut every ~1.2 s; hold 1.5 s on "So he becomes the piglet." and after "Rejected."
- **SFX:** the GULP opens and returns at 0:32 (a callback); stamp THUD on Rejected; oink + sad trombone for the piglets; bass hit on the inflamed stomach; applause for the Nobel; typewriter then silence at the end.
- **Music:** quirky pizzicato/bassoon comedy bed (like a lab-heist) → drops out at "Rejected" and "becomes the piglet" → triumphant swell at the Nobel → dead stop on the last word.
- **Captions:** 1–2 words, bold white, lower-middle. Extra text only: "PERTH · EARLY 1980s", "REJECTED", "DAY 0", "2005 · NOBEL PRIZE".
- **Taste:** keep the vomiting comedic and non-graphic (green face, stink lines). Label the microscope and bacteria shots "illustration".
- **Loop:** the last frame (a doctor hiding behind paper) matches the first frame's composition (a doctor behind a beaker).
