# The Jar That Felt an Earthquake Nobody Else Could - Shorts Package

Format: **True Mechanism Parable** (default) · 2026-11-19 · Research: `research.md`.
Same story as the 35.1M-view reference Short R1, told as the historical record tells it. R1 invented the inventor's exile and the
palace in rubble. The real version has its own doubt-then-vindication arc and needs no invention.

## A. Story research
- **Period:** Eastern Han China; device 132 CE, detection c. 138 CE · **Characters:** Zhang Heng (court astronomer); the capital's scholars
- **Summary:** A bronze seismoscope dropped a ball though nobody in Luoyang felt a tremor. Scholars doubted it; days later a courier
  reported a major earthquake in Longxi, hundreds of km away, and everyone acknowledged the device. Its inner mechanism is still debated.
- **Verified:** design; the drop with no felt quake; the doubt; the courier days later; Longxi quake; everyone convinced. **Disputed:** distance, exact mechanism, working replicas.
- **Sources:** *Hou Hanshu* (primary), Wikipedia, Ancient Pages, Jeremiah Jenne, Xataka (see research.md).

## C. Final voiceover script (212 words · ~61 s at 3.5 words/s)
<!-- VOICEOVER START -->
He built a bronze jar covered in dragons and told the emperor's court it could detect earthquakes.
A jar. That feels earthquakes.
You see, this is China, almost two thousand years ago, and the inventor is Zhang Heng, the court astronomer.
Eight dragons ring the jar, each holding a bronze ball in its mouth.
Under every dragon sits a bronze toad, mouth wide open, waiting.
If the earth moves, a ball drops, and the dragon it fell from points toward the shaking.
Then one day, a ball drops into a toad's mouth.
But in the capital, nothing.
No shaking. No cracks. Nobody felt a thing.
And the scholars of the capital start asking the obvious question. Where's the earthquake?
One day passes. Then two. Then several.
The court astronomer's jar is starting to look like the world's fanciest false alarm.
Then a messenger rides in from the west.
A massive earthquake has hit Longxi, hundreds of kilometers away.
The jar had felt it days before the news arrived.
And every doubter in the capital had to admit the jar was right.
So what was inside it? The old texts mention a central column, and almost nothing else.
Engineers have been building replicas for decades.
They still argue about how it actually worked.
<!-- VOICEOVER END -->

## E. Editing direction
- **Pace:** new image every ~1.2 s (reference measured 1.1–1.3 s). Never hold more than 2 s except the "nothing" beat (hold 2.5 s on purpose).
- **SFX:** metal ting on balls; one big CLANG on the drop, then near-silence for "nothing"; three taps for the days; hooves and taiko for the messenger; choir swell on vindication; a dead stop at the end.
- **Music:** Han-flavoured but modern (guzheng + taiko + pizzicato). Drops out at the drop and at the end.
- **Captions:** 1–2 words, bold white, lower-middle. No other text except the era stamp, the day counter and the map label.
- **Labels:** cutaway diagram tagged "illustration"; description notes "stylised dramatization".
- **Loop:** the last frame (the jar) matches the first frame's composition so the replay feels seamless.
