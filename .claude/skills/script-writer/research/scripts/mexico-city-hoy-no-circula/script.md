# Why Banning Cars Put More Cars on the Road - Shorts Package

Format: **True Mechanism Parable** · 2026-11-19 · From IDEA CARD (idea-creator batch 2026-11-19-mixed-2 #9) · Research: `research.md` (GO WITH QUALIFIERS)

## A. Story research
- **Period:** 1989-2008 · **Place:** Mexico City · **Characters:** the city government; economists Eskeland & Feyzioglu (1997) and Lucas Davis (2008)
- **Summary:** From 20 Nov 1989, each car was banned from driving one weekday by the last digit of its plate. Made permanent in 1990. Two peer-reviewed studies found no air-quality gain and evidence of more driving, more registrations and an older, dirtier fleet, partly because some households bought an extra car.
- **Verified:** dates, plate rule, one-in-five, permanence, Davis's no-improvement finding, registrations/older fleet, "some households bought an additional car".
- **Attributed:** fuel sales didn't fall. **Disputed:** how many families bought a second car (script says "some"). **Avoided:** UN ranking, dead birds, "made the air worse".
- **Sources:** World Bank Economic Review 1997, Journal of Political Economy 2008, Scientific Reports 2017, RFF, Chilango, Wikipedia (leads).

## C. Final voiceover script v4 (235 words · ~63 s at the references' 3.7 words/s)
<!-- VOICEOVER START -->
He bought a rusty old car just so he could keep driving his good one.
His wife watches it cough black smoke across the driveway.
"We already have a car."
You see, it's 1989, and Mexico City is drowning in smog. So the city makes a rule. Hoy No Circula. Today, you don't drive.
It goes by your licence plate. His ends in a five. No driving on Mondays.
The clunker's ends in a seven.
The city says one car in five will vanish every weekday. Just for the winter.
By 1990, it's permanent.
So every Monday, he climbs into the clunker, and it backfires so loud the whole street jumps.
"You're going to kill us all with that thing."
On Tuesday, the clunker's banned. So he takes the good one.
He hasn't missed a single day.
And he's not the only one.
Fuel sales don't drop. Then registrations go up. Then the cars get older. And dirtier.
Years later, economist Lucas Davis checks the city's air monitors, hour by hour.
No evidence the ban cleaned the air at all.
Because the rule never banned a driver. It banned a plate.
So you didn't need to drive less. You just needed a second plate.
And the cheapest way to get one was the dirtiest car on the lot.
The city wanted one car in five off the road.
In his driveway, it got one more.

## E. Editing direction
- **Pace:** a cut every ~1.2 s; hold 1 s of silence on "And he's not the only one." before the crane-up.
- **SFX:** the clunker's cough/backfire is the running gag (open, Monday, the "dirtier" beat, last frame); stamp on MONDAY; flatline tone on "no evidence".
- **Music:** silence → plucked note → comic bassoon for the clunker scenes → dead stop at "not the only one" → tension under the data → stops dead on "more".
- **Captions:** 1–2 words, bold white, lower-middle. Extra text only: "MEXICO CITY · 1989", "5 = NO MONDAYS", "1 IN 5", "1990", "NO IMPROVEMENT". Small "Dramatization" tag on the character shots.
- **Taste/accuracy:** never show a "most polluted city" headline or the air getting *worse*.
- **Loop:** the last shot is the opening driveway, so the end flows back into the hook.
