# Research - "Two lives" (Telugu motivational, anchored on Bronnie Ware)

Owner request (2026-11-27): rewrite their Telugu motivational voiceover ("మనందరికీ రెండు జీవితాలు ఉంటాయి…") with this skill.
This channel's skill requires a real, traceable story, so the quote is anchored on a documented one: palliative carer
Bronnie Ware's record of what dying patients regretted.

## 10 questions (short)
1. What happened? Bronnie Ware, an Australian who worked in palliative care, wrote down the regrets that dying patients shared
   with her. She published them as a blog post (*Inspiration and Chai*), then as the book *The Top Five Regrets of the Dying*
   (2011; some sources say 2012).
2. The documented doubt/mockery? None. This is a reflective story, not a reversal one.
3. The reversal? The regrets were about how people lived, not about what they got: mostly courage, feelings, friends and happiness.
4. The mechanism? Patients near the end looked back and saw choices they had put off.
5. Famous name? "Top five regrets of the dying" is widely searched. The name Bronnie Ware is less well known.

## Evidence buckets
- **VERIFIED (several sources):** Ware worked in palliative care. She published the five regrets: (1) wish I'd had the courage
  to live a life true to myself, not the life others expected; (2) wish I hadn't worked so hard; (3) wish I'd had the courage to
  express my feelings; (4) wish I'd stayed in touch with my friends; (5) wish I'd let myself be happier. The blog came first,
  then the book.
- **REPORTED (her own writing, as widely quoted):** (1) was the most common. (2) was said by almost every male patient she
  nursed, who said they had missed their children's youth. Many suppressed their feelings to keep the peace. Many did not
  realise until the end that happiness is a choice. She cared for patients in their last weeks of life (often quoted as the
  last twelve weeks).
- **DISPUTED / LEGEND:** The line "We have two lives, and the second begins when you realise you only have one" is widely
  credited to Confucius with no source text. It matches the title of Raphaëlle Giordano's 2015 French novel. → In the
  voiceover it is never credited to anyone, and is presented as the owner's own framing.
- **Limitation:** These are anecdotal observations from one carer, not a study. The script says "she noticed", never
  "science shows".
