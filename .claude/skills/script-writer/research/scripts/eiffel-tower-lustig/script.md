## A. Story research
- **Title:** The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower
- **Period:** 1925 (the Paris con) → 1935 (U.S. arrest) → 1947 (death in federal custody)
- **Main characters:** Victor Lustig (con man, "Count"); André Poisson (Paris scrap dealer); James F. Johnson (U.S. Secret Service agent, source of the 1961 account)
- **Summary:** Lustig, a documented con man and counterfeiter, is reported to have posed as a French official in 1925 and taken money and a bribe from scrap dealer André Poisson for the "right" to demolish the Eiffel Tower. Poisson was reportedly too embarrassed to go to the police; Lustig's second attempt was reported to the police and he escaped. He was never charged over the tower. In 1935 the U.S. Secret Service arrested him for counterfeiting; he escaped a New York federal jail on a bedsheet rope, was recaptured and served time at Alcatraz. He died in federal custody in 1947. The tower story's main source is a 1961 book built on the arresting agent's account.
- **Verified facts:** Lustig's career as a con man/counterfeiter; 1935 Secret Service arrest; bedsheet-rope escape and recapture; Alcatraz; death 11 March 1947.
- **Reported (single-source chain) - attributed in script:** the whole 1925 sale (hotel meeting, Poisson, bribe, flight to Austria, second attempt).
- **Disputed / excluded:** price (70,000 vs 1.2 million francs), number of dealers, "sold it twice" (second attempt failed), cause of death, "apprentice salesman" death certificate.
- **Sources:** Smithsonian Magazine, EBSCO Research Starters, New York Almanack, Mental Floss, HeadStuff, Sinister Crimes, The Hustle, Useless Information, Wikipedia (leads). URLs in `research.md`.

## C. Final voiceover script
<!-- VOICEOVER START -->
In 1925, a man sold the Eiffel Tower.
Not a ticket. Not a souvenir. The tower.
His name was Victor Lustig, a con man who called himself a count.
The story goes, Lustig read that the tower was costing Paris a fortune.
So he printed fake government stationery and invited a handful of scrap dealers to a luxury hotel.
In strict secrecy, he told them the government was tearing the tower down.
Thousands of tons of iron. Highest bid wins.
One dealer, André Poisson, wanted it badly.
He paid for the contract. Then he paid Lustig a bribe on top.
Lustig took the money and fled to Austria.
And then... nothing.
Poisson never went to the police. He was too embarrassed.
So Lustig came back and tried to sell it again.
This time, a dealer called the police. Lustig slipped away.
Ten years later, the Secret Service finally got him. Not for the tower. For counterfeit money.
He escaped jail on a rope of bedsheets. They caught him anyway. Alcatraz.
But here's the twist.
Almost everything we know about the Eiffel Tower sale comes from one book, published fourteen years after Lustig died.
And it's based on the man who arrested him.
So history's most famous con has basically one source. His hunter.
<!-- VOICEOVER END -->

## E. Editing direction
- **Sound effects:** rubber stamp (open/close bookend), paper rustle, printing press, door click (repeated for the second attempt), cash register on "bribe", train whistle, phone ring, foghorn + gulls (Alcatraz), page flips. One full silence at 0:42 ("And then... nothing").
- **Music:** "con-artist caper" pizzicato strings + low piano over a subtle ticking pulse; drops out at 0:42 and 1:06 for the two biggest beats; ends on a single piano note. Keep it under the voice (bed ≈ -20 dB relative to VO).
- **Transitions:** hard cuts on every new fact; match cut press → hotel; *visual rhyme* - the second sales meeting reuses the first meeting's orbit move; the final shot reverses the opening push-in so the loop feels seamless.
- **Text overlays:** year/place stamps, names on first mention, "AGAIN.", the 1947 → 1961 date pair, the source line. "AI reconstruction" tag (small, corner) on all AI scenes of real people/places.
- **Pacing:** ≈ 2.7–2.9 words/s; new visual every 2–4 s; hold 1 extra beat after "nothing" and after "But here's the twist."
- **Visual emphasis:** push-ins on the hook, Poisson's eyes, and the book; dimmer, desaturated grade after the arrest; warm gold grade in Paris.
