## Short factual summary
Victor Lustig was a documented international con man and counterfeiter, arrested by the U.S. Secret Service
in 1935, who escaped a New York federal jail, was recaptured, and served his sentence at Alcatraz before dying in
federal custody in 1947. The famous episode - posing as a French official in 1925 and "selling" the Eiffel Tower
as scrap to dealer André Poisson - is widely reported but traces mainly to one source: the 1961 book *The Man Who
Sold the Eiffel Tower* (Floyd Miller, based on the account of Secret Service agent James F. Johnson). He was never
charged over the tower. The usual "sold it twice" claim is overstated: the second attempt is reported to have failed.

## Evidence buckets
### VERIFIED FACTS (multiple independent sources)
- Lustig was a career con man and counterfeiter. - S2, S5, S7
- Arrested by the U.S. Secret Service in 1935 for counterfeiting. - S2, S5, S7
- Escaped the Federal House of Detention in New York using a rope of bedsheets, posing as a window cleaner; recaptured in Pittsburgh (~27 days later per one source). - S5, S8
- Imprisoned at Alcatraz. - S7, S8
- Died 11 March 1947 in federal custody (Medical Center for Federal Prisoners, Springfield, Missouri). - S2, S8
- His death was not made public until August 1949. - S1

### REPORTED CLAIMS (attribute in script: "according to the Secret Service agent who caught him")
- May 1925, Paris; forged official stationery; meeting at the Hôtel de Crillon. - S1 (Johnson memoir)
- Told dealers the tower was to be demolished and sold as scrap, keeping it confidential. - S1, S3
- André Poisson "won" and paid, plus a bribe; Lustig fled to Austria. - S1, S3
- Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police. - S3, S4
- Lustig returned; second round of dealers; one went to police; Lustig escaped. - S3, S1
- Inspired by a newspaper article on maintenance costs. - S1, S3 (article itself not documented - S6)

### HISTORICAL DISPUTES
- **Price:** 70,000 francs (S1-based reporting) vs 1.2 million francs (biographer Christopher Sandford, per S6). → Script gives no figure.
- **Number of dealers:** ~5 vs 6. → Script says "a handful".
- **"Sold twice":** many titles say so; sources describe the second attempt as failing. → Script: "tried to sell it again".
- **Sentence:** 15 + 5 years (S2) vs "20 years" (S8) - consistent in total.
- **Cause of death:** pneumonia vs brain abscess/tumour. → Script omits.
- **Death-certificate occupation "apprentice salesman":** single source (S7). → Not used as fact.

### POPULAR LEGENDS
- "He sold it twice" (successfully) - not supported.
- Lustig's "Ten Commandments for con men" and the Al Capone $5,000 story - not researched here; omit.

### UNKNOWN
- Whether any French police file exists; Poisson's later life; exact amounts.
