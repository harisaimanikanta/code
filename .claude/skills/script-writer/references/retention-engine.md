# Retention Engine - how to write a Short people can't swipe away

The default writing method for every script (since 2026-11-21). It turns any tiny true topic into a 60–90 s Short whose
**hook creates anticipation, whose explanation never lets retention sag, and whose payoff gives a "click" of satisfaction**.
Research basis and sources: §8. Owner feedback that shaped it: §9.

---

## 1. How a Short is judged (what we're writing against)
- **Viewed vs swiped away** (YouTube Studio): the share of feed impressions where people *stayed* instead of swiping. This is
  decided in the first 1–3 seconds. It is the hook's job. [OFFICIAL metric; Google help 12220281]
- **Average percentage viewed (APV)** and the **retention curve**: how much of the Short people watch. Rewatches count, so APV can
  go above 100%. A loop ending raises it. [OFFICIAL metric; >100% = rewatch per third-party guides]
- YouTube publishes **no official "good" numbers**. Third-party guides suggest ~60–80% APV for ~60 s Shorts; treat as directional.
- Healthy curve: a **small dip in seconds 0–2, then a nearly flat line**, then a bump if people rewatch.
- **Where curves fall** (third-party pattern guides, consistent with craft experience):
  | Drop | Usual cause | Fix in the script |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0–3 s cliff | Hook is vague, slow, needs context, or has no question | §3 hook rules |
  | 5–12 s | Context dump after the hook ("You see, in 1925, in the town of…") | Context ≤ 1 sentence, delivered *inside* the action |
  | Mid-video sag | Repetition, a side plot, an "and then" list, too many names | §4 explanation rules: but/therefore, one thread, re-hooks |
  | Just before the end | The answer came too early, or the ending became predictable | Hold the main answer to the last 15 %; add a second reveal |
  | After the payoff | Talking after the loop is closed | Stop ≤ 2 s after the payoff line |

## 2. The engine in one line
**Open one big question in the first 3 seconds → feed partial answers that open smaller questions → close the big one last, with a
twist the viewer didn't see coming but instantly accepts → stop.**

Why it works:
- **Curiosity = an information gap** (Loewenstein, 1994): people feel a mild "itch" when they notice a gap between what they know and
  want to know, and they act to close it. Curiosity is strongest when the gap is **specific and feels closable** (they know enough to
  care, not so little that they don't). → Hooks must be concrete, not vague.
- **Surprise that resolves feels rewarding**: dopamine neurons respond to outcomes that are better than predicted (reward prediction
  error, Schultz); surprising moments raise engagement, enjoyment and memory (Princeton, Antony et al. 2021, sports viewing). → The
  payoff must beat the viewer's guess, not just confirm it.
- **"But / therefore" beats "and then"** (Trey Parker & Matt Stone): each beat should be caused by, or complicate, the one before.
  Causality is what makes a list of facts feel like a story the viewer must follow to the end.

## 3. The hook (0–3 s) - must create anticipation
A 10/10 hook makes the viewer **ask a specific question they can't answer yet**, and **believe the video will answer it**.

### Hook rules
1. **One sentence, ≤ 15 words** (second sentence optional, ≤ 10 words). Readable as on-screen text with the sound off.
2. **Concrete nouns** (a floppy disk, a phone call, one contract) beat abstract ones (a mistake, a secret, a mystery).
3. **Contains the gap**: an outcome without its cause, a cause without its outcome, a contradiction, or a direct question.
4. **No context first**: no year, place or name before the gap appears. They come *after*, inside the story.
5. **No bait**: never "you won't believe", "watch till the end". The gap must be real and paid off.
6. **True**: every word must survive the research file. An inaccurate hook can't score above 0.

### Hook archetypes (generate at least 8, from at least 5 archetypes, every time)
| # | Archetype | Template | Example (true) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | **How-question** | "How did [unlikely actor] [impossible result] with only [tiny means]?" | "How did a killer get caught by a floppy disk he asked the police about?" |
| H2 | **Why-question** | "Why would [normal person] [do absurd thing]?" | "Why would a manager strip-search her own employee because of a phone call?" |
| H3 | **Outcome first** | "[Shocking result]. [It took only X.]" | "A family doctor killed about 250 patients. One typewriter caught him." |
| H4 | **Contradiction** | "[True thing A], but [true thing B that can't fit]." | "The money was real. That was the problem." |
| H5 | **Countdown / anticipation** | "[Person] had [time] before [stakes]." | "He had minutes to decide if five nuclear missiles were real." |
| H6 | **Cold-open action** | "He/She [physical, absurd act]." + one-line why-gap | "He wrote to the police: can a floppy disk be traced?" |
| H7 | **Direct "you"** | "If you [ordinary act], you [hidden consequence]." | "If you've ever sent a Word file, you've left the clue that caught a serial killer." |
| H8 | **The one detail** | "Everything hinged on one [tiny thing]." | "Seventeen years of manhunt ended with one sentence about cake." |
| H9 | **Wrong-belief** | "Everyone believed [X]. It was [Y]." | "Everyone thought the funhouse dummy was wax." |
| H10 | **Mini-list promise** | "[Number] [things]. The [last] one [did the impossible]." | "Three clues. The last one was hidden inside the file." |

### Hook score (each 0–2, total /10) - pitch only 10/10, revise anything lower
| Criterion | 2 = full marks |
|---|---|
| **Gap** | A specific question forms in the viewer's head within 2 s |
| **Concrete** | A picture you can imagine instantly; mostly nouns/verbs |
| **Stakes** | Someone could lose something big (life, money, freedom, a war, a reputation) |
| **Promise** | It's obvious the video will answer the question (and it does) |
| **Speed + truth** | ≤ 15 words, no context first, fully accurate |

## 4. The explanation (3 s → last 15 %) - retention must stay flat
A 10/10 explanation feels like the viewer is **solving it alongside you**, never being lectured.

### Explanation rules
1. **One thread, ≤ 3 named people.** Everyone else is a role ("a TV station", "his pastor").
2. **But / therefore chain.** Every sentence is caused by, or complicates, the one before. Read it with "and then" between lines:
   if it still works, it's a list; rewrite it.
3. **Re-hook every 8–12 s** (about every 30–45 words): a mini-question, a reversal or a number that opens a *smaller* gap
   ("So he asked the police a question.", "There was one problem.", "Then they found the second clue."). Never fake one.
4. **Context in half-sentences, inside the action.** ✅ "Wichita police had chased him for 30 years, so…" ❌ "To understand this, we
   need to go back to 1974."
5. **New, concrete information every 3–5 s.** Numbers spoken the way people say them. One idea per sentence, 4–14 words.
6. **Name the mechanism plainly before you show it** ("Here's the trick:"), so the viewer knows what to watch for.
7. **The viewer should be one step ahead once, and one step behind once.** Ahead = pride ("you can guess what he does next"),
   behind = surprise (the reveal). Both keep people watching.
8. **No repetition** of a fact already said, except the deliberate callback at the end.

### Explanation score (each 0–2, total /10)
| Criterion | 2 = full marks |
|---|---|
| **Clarity** | A 12-year-old could retell it after one listen; ≤ 3 names |
| **Causality** | Every beat is "but" or "therefore"; zero "and then" |
| **Re-hooks** | A new gap opens at least every ~12 s, none faked |
| **Density** | New concrete info every 3–5 s; no sentence can be cut without loss |
| **Mechanism** | The "how" is understandable in one sentence and is shown, not only told |

## 5. The payoff (last 10–15 %) - the "click"
A 10/10 payoff **answers the exact question from the hook**, with **a detail the viewer didn't predict but immediately accepts**
("of course!"), then **stops**.

### Payoff rules
1. **Close the main loop with the hook's own words** (callback). The hook asked "how did a floppy disk catch him?"; the payoff says
   what was inside the floppy disk.
2. **Surprise + inevitability**: the answer must be both unexpected and obviously right in hindsight. Facts that are true but
   unrelated to the hook are not a payoff.
3. **Second reveal ("twist the knife")**: after the main answer, one more true detail that reframes everything (an irony, a scale
   jump, a consequence, the viewer's own connection). Optional, but it's what turns 8/10 into 10/10.
4. **Last line ≤ 12 words**, punchy, ideally turning it on the viewer or flowing back into the first line (loop).
5. **Stop within 2 s.** No moral lecture, no CTA, no "and that's why".

### Payoff score (each 0–2, total /10)
| Criterion | 2 = full marks |
|---|---|
| **Answers the hook** | The exact question from 0:00 is answered, in its own words |
| **Surprise** | The viewer couldn't have guessed it at 0:10 |
| **Inevitability** | In hindsight it was set up earlier (a planted detail) |
| **Second reveal** | A final true detail reframes the story or hits the viewer personally |
| **Clean exit** | Last line ≤ 12 words, ends ≤ 2 s after the answer, can loop |

## 6. Length and pacing
- **60–90 s** (owner's range). At the measured narration pace of **3.5–3.7 words/s**: **210–330 words**. Aim for ~70 s (≈ 250 words)
  unless the story needs more. Longer only if every extra line is a re-hook or the second reveal.
- Rough beat budget for 75 s: hook 0–3 s · first answer + new gap 3–10 s · build with 3–5 re-hooks 10–60 s · main answer 60–70 s ·
  second reveal + last line 70–75 s.

## 7. Retention simulation (mandatory before delivering)
Write a second-by-second "why would they swipe here?" table. For every ~5 s window, name the **open question** keeping the viewer.
If any window has no open question, or two windows in a row only add context, rewrite those lines.

| Time | Line | Open question in the viewer's head | Risk of swipe (L/M/H) | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then score Hook /10, Explanation /10, Payoff /10 with the rubrics above. **Deliver only when all three are 10/10.** Score
honestly: list every point lost and fix it. Scores are editorial judgement against these rubrics, **not view predictions**.
Run `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/retention_check.py <script.md>` as a mechanical lint before the human-style simulation.

## 7a. The three-draft loop and the drop-off audit (mandatory)
Owner rule (2026-11-21): every script is **written 3 times and audited 2 times** before the user sees it.

**Draft 1 → Audit 1 → Draft 2 → Audit 2 → Draft 3 (final) → final check.** Each audit is done from scratch, as a viewer seeing
the Short for the first time, with the thumb over the screen ready to swipe. Each rewrite fixes **every** finding; a rewrite may
change structure, not only words. If the final check still finds a problem, keep looping (Draft 4…).

### Drop-off audit checklist (run on every draft; record each finding as line · second · problem · fix)
**Hook (0–3 s)**
- [ ] Does a specific question form in 2 s? Could a stranger say what the question is?
- [ ] Any word before the gap appears (date, place, name, "So,", "You see")? Cut it.
- [ ] Is it ≥ 16 words, abstract, or does it give away the answer?
- [ ] Is the hook 100% supported by research.md?

**The 3–12 s danger zone**
- [ ] Does line 2 add a *new* gap or raise stakes, instead of explaining background?
- [ ] More than one sentence of context in a row? Merge it into the action.

**Middle (12 s → last 15%)** - the drop-off hunt
- [ ] Any ~12 s window (≈ 45 words) where no new question, twist or number appears? (lint: "longest stretch")
- [ ] Any "and then" sequence or a list of events without but/therefore?
- [ ] Any repeated fact, side plot, or detail the payoff doesn't need?
- [ ] More than 3 named people, or a pronoun whose owner is unclear ("he" = which man?)?
- [ ] Any sentence over 18 words, or a word a 12-year-old wouldn't know?
- [ ] Does the viewer ever have to *remember* something from 30 s ago without a reminder?
- [ ] Is the answer leaked before the final 15%?

**Payoff (last 10–15%)**
- [ ] Does it answer the hook's exact question, in the hook's own words?
- [ ] Would a viewer have guessed it at 0:10? (If yes, it's not a surprise.)
- [ ] Is there a planted detail earlier that makes it feel inevitable?
- [ ] Is there a second reveal, and is it true?
- [ ] Anything after the last strong line? Last line ≤ 12 words?

**Whole script**
- [ ] 60–90 s at 3.5–3.7 words/s? Read it aloud in your head: any tongue-twisters or rhythm stalls?
- [ ] Every claim in a research bucket; qualifiers kept; no invented people, dialogue, events or numbers?

Then the full text of Draft 1 and Draft 2, each followed by its audit findings (line · second · problem · fix).

## 9. Owner feedback log (what "good" means for this channel)
- 2026-11-19: invented characters are not allowed, even as composites. Real people or none.
- 2026-11-20: "unclear" scripts get swiped → plain words, ≤ 3 names, say the trick out loud.
- 2026-11-21 (b): every script must be written 3 times and audited 2 times (§7a) before delivery.
- 2026-11-21: hooks scoring 5–7/10 aren't good enough. Every script must open with **a question or anticipation**, keep the
