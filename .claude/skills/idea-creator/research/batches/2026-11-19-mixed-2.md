Title: The Diaries That Were Bound in Thread From the Future
Category: con
Era & place: 1980-1983, Hamburg, West Germany
Cold open: "He handed a reporter a black notebook stamped with gold initials and said: Hitler wrote this."
True story: Forger Konrad Kujau produced around 60 volumes of fake "Hitler diaries", which Stern journalist Gerd Heidemann bought for the magazine. Stern announced them in April 1983 as a world scoop. Less than two weeks later the West German Bundesarchiv, with federal forensic experts, called them a crude forgery.
Doubt beat: the reverse of the usual: experts and a famous historian initially vouched for them, while the archive's tests were pending.
Reversal: lab tests on the paper, binding and glue showed post-war materials; Kujau and Heidemann were convicted.
Mechanism: the materials betrayed the date: polyester binding thread, post-war glue and paper whitener did not exist in 1945.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (20 on-topic Shorts; top 1,723 views; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Hitle-Diaries ; https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/dokumente-zur-zeitgeschichte/auftakt-des-prozesses-um-die-gefaelschten-hitler-tagebuecher/ ; https://www.thejc.com/on-this-day-hitler-diaries-revealed-as-hoax-mhwvoomi
Watch out: the price paid (often given as ~9 million DM) and the "FH instead of AH" initials story need checking; the whitener date varies (1954 vs 1955); the historian's endorsement should be quoted carefully.
Fresh angle: tell it as forensic science: the thread, not the handwriting, caught him.

Title: The Shot That Made Pinball Legal
Category: trial
Era & place: 1942-1976, New York City
Cold open: "He leaned over a pinball machine in City Hall and told the council exactly where the ball would land."
True story: New York City banned pinball from 1942 to 1976 as gambling. In 1976 writer and player Roger Sharpe was asked to show the City Council that pinball is a game of skill. He called a shot into a specific lane and made it; the council lifted the ban.
Doubt beat: officials treated pinball as a game of chance; the hearing was set up to test exactly that.
Reversal: one called shot, and the 34-year ban ended (other cities followed; Oakland only in 2014).
Mechanism: the legal line between chance (gambling) and skill (legal game): calling a shot proves control.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (19 on-topic Shorts; top 85,479 views; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Sharpe_(pinball) ; https://aeon.co/ideas/pinball-was-an-early-victim-of-america-s-war-on-gambling ; https://atlasobscura.com/articles/the-war-on-pinball
Watch out: exact date (April vs May 1976) varies; Sharpe has said the shot was partly luck, so don't claim it was guaranteed; La Guardia's machine-smashing raids are a separate, true cold-open option.
Fresh angle: the confession that the called shot was a gamble, inside a hearing about gambling.

Title: The Killer Caught by a Radio Wave
Category: other (crime / technology)
Era & place: 1910, London and the Atlantic
Cold open: "He shaved off his moustache, dressed his girlfriend as a boy, and boarded a ship to Canada."
True story: After human remains were found in the cellar of Dr Hawley Crippen's London house, he fled with Ethel Le Neve, disguised as "Mr Robinson and son", on the SS Montrose. Captain Henry Kendall recognised them from newspaper photos and had a wireless message sent to Britain. Inspector Walter Dew took the faster SS Laurentic, got ahead of them and boarded at Father Point disguised as a pilot.
Doubt beat: the fugitives believed a ship at sea was beyond reach; the captain's suspicion grew from small details (the "boy's" behaviour).
Reversal: the detective was waiting for them before they landed; Crippen was hanged in November 1910.
Mechanism: ship-board wireless telegraphy; the news travelled faster than the ship, a first for a manhunt.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: low (14 on-topic Shorts; top 13,994 views; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://atlasobscura.com/articles/the-modern-manhunt-began-with-an-arrest-by-wireless-telegraph ; https://rmg.co.uk/stories/blog/library-archive/henry-kendalls-extra-masters-certificate-capture-dr-crippen ; https://historybytheyard.co.uk/crippen.htm
Watch out: modern DNA work has questioned whether the cellar remains were Cora Crippen's; say "remains found in his cellar", not that he was proven guilty by modern standards. Le Neve was acquitted.
Fresh angle: tell it as a race between a ship and a radio signal.

Title: The Fairies That Fooled Sherlock Holmes's Creator
Category: hoax
Era & place: 1917-1983, Cottingley, Yorkshire
Cold open: "She pinned paper fairies to a bush with hatpins and photographed her cousin beside them."
True story: In 1917 cousins Elsie Wright and Frances Griffiths took photographs of "fairies". Arthur Conan Doyle published them in The Strand Magazine in 1920 and wrote a 1922 book defending them. In the early 1980s the cousins admitted the fairies were cardboard cut-outs copied from a children's book.
Doubt beat: sceptics doubted the photos; Doyle and photo experts of the time backed them.
Reversal: the creator of the most logical detective in fiction was fooled by two children for the rest of his life.
Mechanism: cut-outs held up with hatpins; the photos were never "doctored", which is why plate examiners found no tampering.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (17 on-topic Shorts; 3 with 100K+ views, top 4,885,933; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search). One very big Short exists.
Evidence: https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/Doyle_and_the_Fairies ; https://hoaxes.org/photo_database/image/the_cottingley_fairies/ ; https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/sites/default/files/ebook/article/cottingley_fairies-742.pdf
Watch out: confession date (1981/1982/1983) varies; Frances always said the fifth photo was real; the source book (1914 vs 1915) varies.
Fresh angle: why the experts found no tampering: there was nothing to tamper with.

Title: The Spacecraft Killed by a Pound
Category: engineering
Era & place: 1998-1999, NASA / Mars
Cold open: "He fired the thrusters, wrote the number down, and forgot to say it was in pounds."
True story: Mars Climate Orbiter was lost on 23 September 1999 as it arrived at Mars. Thruster data was produced in pound-force seconds while the navigation software expected newton-seconds, so each firing was underestimated by a factor of about 4.45. The trajectory drifted until the probe passed far too low into the atmosphere.
Doubt beat: the investigation found navigators had noticed the discrepancy and their concerns were not acted on; a planned correction burn (TCM-5) was not done.
Reversal: a $125M spacecraft lost to unit labels.
Mechanism: 1 lbf = 4.45 N; small errors on every firing add up across months of flight.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 1, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: low (13 on-topic Shorts; 1 with 100K+ views, top 185,779; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: NASA Mars Climate Orbiter Mishap Investigation Board Phase I report (1999) ; https://homepages.uc.edu/~thomam/Implications_IT/pdf/ch8/mars_orbiter_failure.pdf
Watch out: the closest-approach altitude figure varies; attribute the units file per the official report; the cost figure depends on what's included.
Fresh angle: pair the cold open with the ignored navigators.

Title: The Doctor Who Stole a Pump Handle
Category: doubted invention (medical)
Era & place: 1854, Soho, London
Cold open: "He walked to the busiest water pump in Soho and asked the town to take off its handle."
True story: During the 1854 Broad Street cholera outbreak, Dr John Snow mapped deaths and found they clustered around one pump. The parish board was sceptical but removed the handle on 8 September 1854. Later a leaking cesspool next to the well was identified.
Doubt beat: officials followed miasma theory (bad air); the General Board of Health rejected Snow's water theory.
Reversal: the waterborne theory became the foundation of modern epidemiology.
Mechanism: cholera spreads through contaminated water; the deaths map showed the source.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 1, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: low (16 on-topic Shorts; top 41,194 views; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5334a1.htm ; https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/john-snow-cholera-broad-street-pump/ ; https://epi-snow.ph.ucla.edu/
Watch out: the outbreak was already declining when the handle came off; don't claim the handle alone ended it. The brewery workers who drank beer and survived are a popular detail; verify.
Fresh angle: the map as the "mechanism" image.

Title: The Jet That Ran Out of Fuel at 41,000 Feet
Category: engineering
Era & place: 23 July 1983, Manitoba, Canada
Cold open: "He looked at his gauges at 41,000 feet and both engines went silent."
True story: Air Canada Flight 143, a new Boeing 767, ran out of fuel mid-flight. With the fuel gauges inoperative, the fuel load was calculated by hand using the wrong conversion (pounds instead of kilograms), so the plane carried about half the fuel needed. The crew glided to a former RCAF base at Gimli and landed with no deaths.
Doubt beat: the calculation was checked several times and passed, because everyone used the same wrong factor.
Reversal: the plane landed safely on a disused runway.
Mechanism: kg vs lb (factor ~2.2) during Canada's metric changeover.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: medium (11 on-topic Shorts; 4 with 100K+ views, top 1,952,355; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.cbc.ca/archives/when-a-metric-mix-up-led-to-the-gimli-glider-emergency-1.4754039 ; https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Gimli_Glider ; Transport Canada board of inquiry report (1985)
Watch out: altitude and headcount vary by source; the sideslip and glider-pilot details need the official report; the runway being used for a car-racing event that day is widely reported but check it.
Fresh angle: the "Swiss cheese": a broken gauge plus a new unit system plus a double-checked wrong number.

Title: The Professor Who Found His Own Name in a Rock
Category: hoax
Era & place: 1725-1726, Würzburg
Cold open: "He dug a stone out of a hillside, and on it was a carved spider still sitting in its web."
True story: Professor Johann Beringer collected hundreds of "fossils" from Mount Eibelstadt showing spiders, frogs, stars and Hebrew letters, and published them in a 1726 book. They had been carved and planted; colleagues J. Ignatz Roderick and Georg von Eckhart were implicated in a later court case.
Doubt beat: the stones looked too perfect; Beringer published anyway.
Reversal: the hoax was exposed; the hoaxers lost their posts and Beringer's reputation suffered.
Mechanism: soft limestone carved and planted where he'd dig.
Fit score: 8/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 1, evidence 2)
Competition: low (6 on-topic Shorts; top 3,108 views; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Beringer ; https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/history-of-geology/frauds-fakes-and-fossils/ ; https://depositsmag.com/2018/03/29/fake-fossils-by-the-hundred-the-story-of-johann-beringers-lying-stones/
Watch out: the famous "stone with his own name on it" and "bought back every copy" endings are disputed/legendary; who carved the first stones is uncertain, and some argue Beringer was involved.
Fresh angle: present the legend endings honestly as legend.

Title: Why Banning Cars Put More Cars on the Road
Category: backfired plan
Era & place: 1989-2008, Mexico City
Cold open: "Every family was banned from driving one day a week, so they bought another car."
True story: In 1989 Mexico City introduced Hoy No Circula, banning each car from driving one weekday based on its licence plate. Economist Lucas Davis (Journal of Political Economy, 2008) found no measurable improvement in air quality and evidence that the number of vehicles rose.
Doubt beat: the policy was popular and widely copied by other cities.
Reversal: more, often older and dirtier, second cars.
Mechanism: people adapted: a second car with a different plate gets around the ban.
Fit score: 8/10 (action 1, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: low (20 Shorts match the search, but most are news about the current rules; 3 with 100K+ views, top 439,650; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/ldavis/Davis%20JPE%202008.pdf ; https://www.resources.org/common-resources/driving-restriction-and-air-quality-in-mexico-city/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5289101
Watch out: "bought a second car" is an inference from registrations and sales; say "the evidence suggests". Don't claim it made air worse overall.
Fresh angle: almost nobody tells the economics.

Title: The Chicken That Lived Without a Head
Category: medical
Era & place: 1945-1947, Fruita, Colorado
Cold open: "The farmer swung his axe, and the chicken got up and walked back to the coop."
True story: On 10 September 1945 farmer Lloyd Olsen beheaded a rooster for dinner, but it survived. "Mike" lived about 18 months, fed through his oesophagus with a dropper, and toured as a sideshow. University of Utah scientists examined him.
Doubt beat: people assumed it was a trick; Olsen took Mike to the University of Utah to prove it.
Reversal: scientists confirmed it was real.
Mechanism: the axe missed the jugular, a clot stopped the bleeding, and enough of the brain stem remained to run breathing, heart rate and reflexes.
Fit score: 8/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 1, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (15 on-topic Shorts; 1 with 100K+ views, top 2,767,343; checked 2026-11-19, yt-dlp search). One very big Short exists.
Evidence: https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/running-ponies/meet-miracle-mike-the-chicken-who-lived-for-18-months-without-his-head/ ; https://content.time.com/time/specials/packages/article/0,28804,1989200_1989201,00.html ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/strange_planet_mike_the_headle
Watch out: the pickled head / cat stories are weakly sourced; date (April vs September 1945) varies; "brain stem" is the period explanation, refine with modern anatomy.
Fresh angle: the anatomy (most of a chicken's brain sits at the back of the head).
