Title: The Doctor Who Pushed a Tube Into His Own Heart
Category: medical
Era & place: 1929, Eberswalde, Germany
Cold open: "He cut open his own arm and pushed a 65-centimetre tube toward his heart."
True story: Surgical resident Werner Forssmann was refused permission for the experiment, so he did it on himself. He threaded a catheter through an arm vein into his right atrium, walked to the X-ray room and photographed it. In 1956 he shared the Nobel Prize for cardiac catheterisation.
Doubt beat: his superior forbade it; the medical view was that entering the heart would be fatal
Reversal: an X-ray shows the catheter in his heart and he's fine; Nobel Prize 27 years later
Mechanism: veins run continuously to the heart, so a flexible tube can follow them in without piercing anything
Fit score: 10/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: unknown (not checked 2026-11-26, yt-dlp missing)
Evidence: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1956/forssmann/biographical/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2178572/ ; https://www.britannica.com/biography/Werner-Forssmann
Watch out: whether and how he was fired is disputed (a 2002 paper asks "legend or reality"); the nurse-tied-to-table detail needs checking
Fresh angle: the method then sat unused for ~10 years until American doctors picked it up

Title: The Man Who Stopped From 632 mph in One Second
Category: other (science)
Era & place: 10 Dec 1954, Holloman AFB, New Mexico
Cold open: "He strapped himself to a rocket, hit 632 miles per hour, then slammed into water."
True story: Air Force flight surgeon John Paul Stapp rode the Sonic Wind No. 1 rocket sled himself. It braked from 632 mph in about 1.4 s, about 46.2 g. His tests shaped seat-belt and ejection standards.
Doubt beat: the prevailing view was that humans couldn't survive about 18 g
Reversal: he survives 46.2 g (with temporary eye damage)
Mechanism: forward-facing restraint spreads the force across the body, so g tolerance is far higher than thought
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: unknown (not checked 2026-11-26)
