IDEA CARD
Title: The Phone Call That Made a Manager Strip-Search Her Employee
Category: con
Era & place: 9 April 2004, Mount Washington, Kentucky, USA
Cold open: "She picked up the restaurant phone, and a man said he was a police officer."
True story: A caller claiming to be a police officer convinced a McDonald's assistant manager, Donna Summers, that 18-year-old employee Louise Ogborn had stolen from a customer, and directed her and others to search and detain Ogborn in a back room for hours. It was a hoax; similar calls had been made to restaurants across the US for years.
Doubt beat: none of the adults involved questioned the caller's authority for hours; a maintenance worker finally refused, which helped end it.
Reversal: the "officer" was a hoaxer; Ogborn won about $6.1M against McDonald's in 2007 (upheld on appeal); Walter Nix got 5 years; suspect David Stewart was acquitted.
Mechanism: obedience to perceived authority: a confident voice, a uniform-free "badge" over the phone, and small escalating requests (foot-in-the-door).
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2) - doubt is inverted: the horror is that nobody doubted.
Competition: low (6 on-topic Shorts; 2 with 100K+ views, top 724,144; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://abcnews.go.com/2020/story?id=3688563 ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/mcdonalds-strip-search-victim-gets-61m ; https://www.courthousenews.com/court-upholds-6m-award-in-strip-search-case/
Watch out: sexual abuse of a real, living victim: no graphic detail, no sensational visuals, focus on the mechanism; the caller was never convicted, so don't name Stewart as the caller.
Fresh angle: tell it as Milgram outside the lab, with the line "similar calls had worked for a decade".

Title: The 38 Witnesses Who Never Existed
Category: other (media myth)
Era & place: 1964-2016, Kew Gardens, New York
Cold open: "A newspaper said 38 people watched a woman being attacked and nobody called the police."
True story: After Kitty Genovese was murdered in March 1964, a New York Times story claimed 38 neighbours watched and did nothing. It launched the "bystander effect". Later research (American Psychologist, 2007) found no evidence of 38 witnesses who saw the attack and stayed passive; in 2016 the Times called its own reporting flawed.
Doubt beat: rival reporters doubted the story in 1964 but didn't challenge it; editor A.M. Rosenthal defended it.
Reversal: the most famous example of apathy in psychology was largely a myth; some neighbours did call or try to help.
Mechanism: a number from a police commissioner over lunch became "fact" through repetition (and the bystander effect itself is real in later experiments).
Fit score: 9/10 (action 1, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 2)
Competition: low (10 on-topic Shorts; 1 with 100K+ views, top 220,607; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/article/critical-thinking-history/bystander-effect-started-lie ; https://www.cjr.org/the_kicker/and_thats_the_way_it_was_march_8.php ; https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Kitty_Genovese
Watch out: a real murder victim; be respectful. Don't claim the bystander effect is fake; the effect has later lab support, only the Genovese story was exaggerated.
Fresh angle: most competitors still tell the "38 witnesses" version.

Title: The Hostage Who Phoned the Prime Minister to Defend Her Captors
Category: crime
Era & place: 23-28 August 1973, Norrmalmstorg, Stockholm
Cold open: "She was held at gunpoint in a bank vault, and she phoned the Prime Minister to complain about the police."
True story: Jan-Erik Olsson took four bank employees hostage; his friend Clark Olofsson was brought in at his demand. During the six-day siege, hostage Kristin Enmark phoned Prime Minister Olof Palme and said she feared the police more than the robbers. Police used gas to end it; everyone survived.
Doubt beat: the public and police couldn't understand why the hostages sided with the robbers.
Reversal: the case gave its name to "Stockholm syndrome".
Mechanism: under threat, small kindness from the captor and fear of a police assault can create attachment and trust.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (20 on-topic Shorts; top 30,060; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norrmalmstorg_robbery ; https://www.history.com/articles/stockholm-syndrome ; https://thestar.com.my/news/focus/2023/09/01/enduring-myth-of-stockholm-syndrome
Watch out: Stockholm syndrome is not an official diagnosis and is disputed; Enmark herself has rejected the label. Rumours of a romance are not established.
Fresh angle: end on Enmark's view that she was reacting rationally to the police, not "brainwashed".

Title: The Shock Machine Half the Volunteers Didn't Believe
Category: science
Era & place: 1961-1962, Yale University
Cold open: "He turned the dial to 450 volts while the man next door screamed."
True story: Stanley Milgram's obedience experiments reported that about 65% of volunteers delivered the maximum "shock" when ordered. Psychologist Gina Perry's study of Milgram's Yale archives (Behind the Shock Machine, 2012) found unpublished variations and evidence that only about half the participants believed the shocks were real.
Doubt beat: some participants openly doubted the set-up; Perry found that doubters were more willing to go higher.
Reversal: the most famous result in psychology may partly measure who saw through the trick.
Mechanism: authority + the shock was fake (an actor); belief changes behaviour.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (17 on-topic Shorts; 2 with 100K+ views, top 1,085,679; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.npr.org/2013/08/28/209559002/taking-a-closer-look-at-milgrams-shocking-obedience-study ; https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/book-review-behind-the-shock-machine/ ; https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/psych-lies-and-audiotape-the-tarnished-legacy-of-the-milgram-shock-experiments/
Watch out: Perry's reading is one researcher's critique; replications still find high obedience. Don't call Milgram "fake".
Fresh angle: the archive twist, not the usual "65%".

Title: The Study That Proved Psychiatrists Can't Spot Sanity, and Might Have Been Faked
Category: science hoax
Era & place: 1969-1973 (published in Science 1973); exposed 2019
Cold open: "He walked into a psychiatric hospital and said one word: thud."
True story: David Rosenhan's 1973 paper said eight sane "pseudopatients" were admitted to psychiatric hospitals after reporting voices saying "empty, hollow, thud", then kept for days to weeks. It reshaped psychiatry. Journalist Susannah Cahalan's investigation (The Great Pretender, 2019) found that Rosenhan likely misrepresented and probably fabricated parts of the data.
Doubt beat: Robert Spitzer attacked the study in 1975 and asked for the data; it was never fully released.
Reversal: a study about doctors being fooled may itself have fooled the world.
Mechanism: labelling: once diagnosed, normal behaviour is read as symptoms; and a famous result went unchecked for 46 years.
Fit score: 9/10 (action 2, doubt 2, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (8 on-topic Shorts; 1 with 100K+ views, top 219,673; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.npr.org/2019/11/04/776036170/the-great-pretender-seeks-the-truth-about-on-being-sane-in-insane-places ; https://www.sciencehistory.org/stories/distillations-pod/the-fraud-that-transformed-psychiatry/ ; https://psychologytoday.com/ie/blog/freud-fluoxetine/201911/the-rosenhan-study-never-proved-anything-anyway
Watch out: "fabricated" is Cahalan's conclusion ("probably"); say "an investigation found evidence he misrepresented the data".
Fresh angle: double twist (the experiment, then the experimenter).

Title: The Harvard Experiment on the Future Unabomber
Category: science / crime
Era & place: 1959-1962, Harvard University
Cold open: "They sat him under bright lights, wired him up, and attacked everything he believed, on camera."
True story: Psychologist Henry Murray recruited 22 Harvard undergraduates, including 17-year-old Ted Kaczynski, for a stress study. Students wrote essays on their personal philosophy, then were attacked over them by an interrogator, filmed, and shown the footage. Murray called the attacks "vehement, sweeping, and personally abusive."
Doubt beat: students had no idea the "debate" was designed to humiliate them.
Reversal: decades later, the quiet student became the Unabomber; Harvard sealed his data in 2000.
Mechanism: deliberate stress interrogation to measure reactions; whether it contributed to his later violence is unknown.
Fit score: 8/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 1, evidence 2)
Competition: low (1 on-topic Short; top 59,925; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.history.com/articles/what-happened-to-the-unabomber-at-harvard ; https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/impromptu-man/201205/harvards-experiment-the-unabomber-class-62 ; https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/62564/how-extreme-psychological-study-may-have-affected-young-ted-kaczynski
Watch out: no evidence of a causal link to his crimes; never say the experiment "created" the Unabomber. Don't glorify him; victims exist.
Fresh angle: almost uncovered on Shorts.

Title: The CIA Safehouse With a Two-Way Mirror
Category: espionage
Era & place: c. 1955-1965, San Francisco (225 Chestnut Street) and New York
Cold open: "He sat on a toilet behind a two-way mirror, drinking martinis and taking notes."
True story: Operation Midnight Climax, part of the CIA's MKUltra program, was run by narcotics officer George Hunter White under the alias Morgan Hall. Sex workers paid by the agency brought men from bars to safehouses, where they were secretly dosed with LSD and observed through two-way mirrors.
Doubt beat: the men had no idea; the program stayed secret until investigations in the 1970s.
Reversal: exposed in 1977 Senate hearings; the safehouses had closed in 1965-66.
Mechanism: testing drugs and interrogation on unknowing subjects to study behaviour and blackmail potential.
Fit score: 8/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 1, evidence 2)
Competition: low (14 on-topic Shorts; top 22,169; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)

Title: The Experiment That Taught Orphans to Stutter
Category: medical
Era & place: 1939, Davenport, Iowa
Cold open: "She told a healthy little girl she was a stutterer, every week, until she was."
True story: Speech pathologist Wendell Johnson's graduate student Mary Tudor told orphans at the Iowa Soldiers' Orphans' Home with normal speech that they were starting to stutter. Several became anxious and withdrawn and their speech suffered. The study stayed hidden until a 2001 San Jose Mercury News investigation.
Doubt beat: colleagues later nicknamed it the "Monster Study" and it was kept quiet for decades.
Reversal: the University of Iowa apologised (2001); in 2007 Iowa paid $925,000 to surviving subjects and estates.
Mechanism: labelling and negative feedback change behaviour and self-image.
Fit score: 8/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (18 on-topic Shorts; top 53,514; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/iowa-pay-subjects-925k-stuttering-study-flna1c9469063 ; https://www.chronicle.com/article/iowa-settles-lawsuit-over-stuttering-study/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_Study
Watch out: whether children became lifelong stutterers is disputed; say the children were harmed (anxiety, self-consciousness), not "turned into stutterers".
Fresh angle: Tudor herself returned to try to undo it.

Title: The Teacher Who Turned His Class Into a Movement in a Week
Category: other (classroom experiment)
Era & place: April 1967, Cubberley High School, Palo Alto, California
Cold open: "He drew a wave on the chalkboard and told his students it was their new symbol."
True story: History teacher Ron Jones couldn't explain how ordinary Germans followed the Nazis, so he started a movement in class: "The Third Wave", with a salute, slogans and members reporting on each other. It spread beyond his class within days before he ended it at a rally.
Doubt beat: the students mocked rules at first, then enforced them.
Reversal: at the final rally Jones showed them a picture of Hitler: they had become what they were studying.
Mechanism: group belonging, discipline and identity override individual judgement.
Fit score: 7/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 2, mechanism 1, evidence 1)
Competition: low (16 Shorts match, none with views; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Third_Wave_(experiment) ; https://n2v.paloaltoonline.com/news/2017/03/17/the-wave-that-changed-history ; https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/ron-jones-the-third-wave-1967-an-account.a4.pdf
Watch out: mostly Jones's own account published years later; numbers and days vary. Former students' documentary "Lesson Plan" (2010) is the best corroboration; check it.

Title: The Steel Box Built to Cause Despair
Category: science
Era & place: c. 1970-1972, University of Wisconsin-Madison
Cold open: "He lowered a young monkey into a steel funnel and waited to see if it would give up."
True story: Psychologist Harry Harlow and his student Stephen Suomi built the "vertical chamber apparatus", which Harlow called the "pit of despair": a steel trough where young rhesus monkeys were isolated to model human depression. Within days the monkeys stopped moving and huddled.
Doubt beat: critics then and since questioned what it proved; Harlow himself was struggling with depression after his wife's death.
Reversal: the experiments fuelled the animal-welfare movement.
Mechanism: isolation and loss of attachment produce depression-like behaviour.
Fit score: 7/10 (action 2, doubt 1, reversal 1, mechanism 2, evidence 1)
Competition: low (10 on-topic Shorts; 2 with 100K+ views, top 121,699; checked 2026-11-20, yt-dlp search)
Evidence: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pit_of_despair ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9305880 ; https://www.iflscience.com/health-and-medicine/the-pit-of-despair-was-one-of-the-most-unethical-experiments-of-modern-science/
Watch out: confinement duration is disputed (weeks vs months); avoid graphic animal-cruelty visuals (YouTube policy).
Fresh angle: the scientist was trying to understand his own depression.
