## Parable-fit pitches - round 1 (2026-11-19, via `references/idea-engine.md`)
Scored with the 5-point fit test. Evidence was checked through search summaries; full research is still required before scripting.

### 1. The Ancient Jar Everyone Thought Was Broken - **scripted** (`scripts/zhang-heng-seismoscope/`)
- Cold open: "He built a bronze jar covered in dragons and told the emperor's court it could detect earthquakes."
- Fit 9/10 · Evidence: Hou Hanshu; https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Zhang_Heng · Risk: the competitor's invented "palace rubble" ending

### 2. The Rat Bounty That Created Rat Farms (Hanoi, 1902)
- Cold open: "He cut the tail off a live rat, let it go, and walked into the government office to get paid."
- Story: French colonial Hanoi paid 1 cent per rat tail to fight plague rats. Officials began seeing tailless rats in the streets, then found rat *farms* in the suburbs.
- Doubt beat: officials celebrate the record tallies (over 20,000 rats on 12 June 1902 alone) · Reversal: the bounty grows the rats · Mechanism: perverse incentive (the "Rat Effect")
- Fit 10/10 · Evidence: Michael G. Vann, *The Great Hanoi Rat Hunt*; https://worldhistoryconnected.press.uillinois.edu/15.2/vann_interview.html ; https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/hanoi-rat-massacre-1902 · Risk: exact tallies vary

### 3. The Doctor Locked Away for Saying "Wash Your Hands"
- Cold open: "He made every doctor in the hospital scrub their hands in bleach, and the death rate collapsed."
- Story: Ignaz Semmelweis cut maternity deaths in Vienna (1847) with chlorinated-lime handwashing. He was rejected and ridiculed, and died in an asylum in 1865, aged 47.
- Doubt: colleagues reject him · Reversal (tragic irony): proven right after his death; germ theory · Mechanism: invisible "cadaverous particles", i.e. germs
- Fit 9/10 · Evidence: https://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2015/01/12/375663920/the-doctor-who-championed-hand-washing-and-saved-women-s-lives ; https://wellcomecollection.org/articles/X9eRxRIAACMA1BZa · Risk: the beating/cause of death is disputed, so say "died in an asylum weeks later"

### 4. The Forger Who Had to Paint a Fake to Save His Life
- Cold open: "He picked up a brush in front of the police to prove he was a criminal."
- Story: after WWII, Han van Meegeren was charged with treason (a capital offence) for selling a "Vermeer" to Hermann Göring. His defence: he had painted it himself. He painted a new "Vermeer" in front of witnesses.
- Doubt: nobody believes a nobody painted Vermeers · Reversal: acquitted of treason, convicted of forgery (1 year) · Mechanism: Bakelite + an oven to age the paint
- Fit 10/10 · Evidence: https://britannica.com/biography/Han-van-Meegeren ; https://www.historyhit.com/nazi-sympathiser-or-national-hero-the-strange-story-of-han-van-meegeren/ · Risk: the painting's title and the date of death differ across sources

### 5. The Country That Killed Its Sparrows
- Cold open: "He climbed onto his roof and banged a pot for hours so no sparrow could land."
- Story: China's Four Pests Campaign (1958) killed sparrows for eating grain. Without them, locusts and other pests surged, worsening the Great Famine. China later imported sparrows from the USSR (reported as 250,000).
- Doubt: the campaign is celebrated · Reversal: harvests fall · Mechanism: sparrows ate locusts
- Fit 8/10 · Evidence: https://climate.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Campaigning-for-Extinction-Eradication-of-Sparrows-and-the-Great-Famine-in-China.pdf ; https://www.voxdev.org/topic/agriculture/four-pests-campaign-and-chinas-great-famine · Risk: the famine had many causes, so don't claim sparrows "caused" it; the import figure needs a source

### 6. The Horse That Could Do Math
- Cold open: "He asked his horse what 3 plus 2 was, and the horse tapped its hoof five times."
- Story: Clever Hans amazed Berlin (~1904). A commission couldn't find a trick. In 1907 psychologist Oskar Pfungst found the horse was reading tiny unconscious cues from the questioner.
- Doubt: experts expect fraud and find none · Reversal: no fraud, and no math either · Mechanism: involuntary body language (the "Clever Hans effect")
- Fit 9/10 · Evidence: https://www.britannica.com/topic/Clever-Hans ; https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/how-counting-horses-and-reading-dogs-convinced-us-animals-could-think · Risk: accuracy percentages vary

### 7. The Bridge That Danced Itself to Death
- Cold open: "Drivers paid a toll to cross a bridge that rolled like the ocean under their cars."
- Story: Tacoma Narrows Bridge ("Galloping Gertie") opened 1 July 1940, bounced in the wind (people drove it for fun), and tore itself apart on 7 Nov 1940 in ~40 mph wind. The only death was a dog left in a car.
- Doubt: the bounce is treated as a novelty · Reversal: collapse in moderate wind · Mechanism: aeroelastic flutter (not simple resonance)
- Fit 9/10 · Evidence: https://www.livescience.com/technology/engineering/science-history-the-tacoma-narrows-bridge-collapses-forcing-a-complete-rethink-in-structural-engineering-nov-7-1940 ; WSDOT history pages · Risk: details about the dog vary

### 8. The Diamond Field That Was Seeded by Hand (1872)
- Cold open: "He scattered diamonds across a desert and invited the richest men in America to come find them."
- Story: Philip Arnold and John Slack salted a remote Western site with real gems. Top financiers and Tiffany's appraisal were fooled. Government geologist Clarence King found cut stones and exposed it.
- Doubt: the investors believe the experts · Reversal: a young geologist finds a *cut* diamond in the dirt · Mechanism: salting (the true version of reference R5)
- Fit 9/10 · Evidence: needs a research pass (Smithsonian / USGS history) · Risk: dollar amounts vary widely

### 9. The Army Made of Balloons
- Cold open: "Four soldiers picked up a 40-ton tank and carried it across the field." (inflatable)
- Story: the U.S. 23rd Headquarters Special Troops ("Ghost Army", WWII) used inflatable tanks, sound trucks and fake radio traffic to impersonate whole divisions.
- Doubt: locals see "tanks" lifted by hand · Reversal: German units misread Allied strength (verify specific operations) · Mechanism: multi-sense deception
- Fit 8/10 · Evidence: needs a research pass (Smithsonian, National WWII Museum); Round 2 shows heavy news coverage, so medium competition · Risk: overstated "it won the war" claims
