# Style Target - what an idea must be able to become

Summary of the script-writer's reference analysis: 5 Shorts, 6.8M–35.1M views, word-timed transcripts and storyboard frames
(full analysis in the script-writer repo: `skills/script-writer/references/reference-style-analysis.md`).

## The format: True Mechanism Parable (~60 s, ~220 words)
1. **Cold-open action (0–4 s):** "He [did something physical, visual and absurd]." No name or date yet.
   e.g. "He loaded the shotgun with gold and fired it into the walls of the mine."
2. **Reaction:** someone laughs or doubts.
3. **"You see,"** - who, where, when, stakes.
4. **Mockery ladder:** 2–3 rounds of ridicule, with time jumps ("Weeks later…", "By July…").
5. **Rule of three:** "Then one. Then another. Then all eight."
6. **Reversal:** the doubted side is proven right (or the plan backfires) at peak stakes.
7. **Mechanism:** the "how", in 2–3 concrete sentences.
8. **Hard cut** on the payoff image. No moral, no CTA.

## So a good idea has
| Needed | Ask |
|---|---|
| A cold-open image | Can the first sentence be "He [did X]" where X is something you can picture and that makes no sense yet? |
| Doubt | Did real people mock, doubt or ignore it? (documented) |
| Reversal | Did reality flip at a dramatic moment? |
| Mechanism | Can the "why/how" be explained physically or psychologically in two sentences? |
| Evidence | Can the central event be sourced to reputable or primary sources? |

Reference titles that worked (mechanism curiosity, not event names): "The Ancient Jar That Warned an Empire", "Why Perfect Railroads
Don't Work", "The Job Nobody Wants in Japan", "The Instinct That Makes You Crash", "How Mine Salting Worked".

Warning: the reference channel invents plot details. This channel doesn't. Ideas must work with the **real** events.
