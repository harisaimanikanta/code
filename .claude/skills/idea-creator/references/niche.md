# Channel Niche - Historical WTF Stories

Real historical events that sound completely fictional. The target reaction: **"WAIT. THIS ACTUALLY HAPPENED?"**

## Wanted (examples define the flavour, not the limits)
- A man who sold the Eiffel Tower (Victor Lustig, 1925)
- A man who invented a country and sold land in it (Gregor MacGregor, Poyais)
- A pope who put a dead pope on trial (Cadaver Synod, 897)
- A woman who convinced doctors she gave birth to rabbits (Mary Toft, 1726)
- A man who passed himself off as a native of Formosa (George Psalmanazar)
- A fake diamond field that fooled financiers (Great Diamond Hoax, 1872)
- A war linked to a stolen wooden bucket (Zappolino, 1325)
- A military operation against emus (1932)
- A corpse used to deceive Nazi intelligence (Operation Mincemeat, 1943)
- A fake fossil that fooled scientists (Piltdown Man)

## Best fits for the channel's format
Stories with a **doubted or mocked person or idea**, a **real reversal**, and a **mechanism** viewers can understand in one sentence:
inventions laughed at then vindicated · plans that backfired · cons with a clever trick · engineering failures with a simple cause ·
official practices that sound fake · military deceptions · scientific frauds and how they were caught.

## Not wanted
Ordinary history lessons, generic biographies, routine explanations, lists of facts, well-known textbook events with no twist,
conspiracy theories, unverifiable legends presented as fact, tragedy without a twist, or stories that need long context.

## Non-negotiable
Real and traceable, supported by credible evidence. Disputed details are flagged. Legends are labelled or rejected.
